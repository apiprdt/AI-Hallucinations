\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we introduced a controlled semantic perturbation framework to evaluate how the semantic orientation of text influences LLM-based molecular property prediction behavior. Our results reveal a robust task-dependent divergence in semantic sensitivity: enzymatic reasoning (BACE) and solubility tasks (ESOL) are robustly fragile to semantic fabrication, characterized by a statistically significant collapse of discriminative capacity and calibration. This ``distributional compression'' systematically forces predictions into a narrow, non-informative confidence state ($D_{KL} > 14$). Conversely, physicochemical prediction (BBBP) exhibits directional sensitivity to structural topic focus, producing moderate effect sizes (Cohen's $d = +0.51$) without destroying prediction entropy. Crucially, we find that the \textit{semantic orientation} induced by a prompt can drive predictive shifts even in the absence of factual taxonomic correctness, suggesting that textual augmentations function as a form of semantic conditioning that modulates the LLM's inference state.

These findings indicate that text-conditioned generation is highly sensitive to the structural claims and topical focus of the prompt, providing a methodological probe for investigating inference behavior. We observe that prompt-conditioned generation did not reliably yield taxonomy-consistent outputs, underscoring the challenge of enforcing specific semantic perturbation categories in generative chemistry systems. This study provides a structured methodological foundation for future work investigating the boundary between linguistic register and semantic utility in text-conditioned molecular prediction.

\subsubsection*{Future directions.} Key extensions include: (1) investigation of the distributional compression phenomenon on additional enzymatic datasets, (2) expansion of multi-model validation across diverse LLM architectures and scales, (3) larger-scale evaluation with cross-validation, and (4) comparison with fine-tuned LLM models and additional traditional ML methods to further contextualize the practical significance of the observed effects.
