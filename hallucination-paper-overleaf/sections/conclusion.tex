\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The primary contribution of this work is a controlled semantic perturbation framework that enables systematic isolation of semantic effects in LLM-based molecular prediction. This exploratory study provides empirical evidence that the type of structured perturbation---not merely its presence---suggests differential outcomes in zero-shot prediction tasks. Through a controlled nine-condition ablation using a heuristic categorization scheme, we observe that prompt-constrained Structural Phantom (SP) augmentations yield the largest observed ROC-AUC increase on the BBBP physicochemical task (0.626 vs.\ 0.533 baseline), although none of the improvements reached statistical significance ($p > 0.05$). Conversely, on the BACE enzymatic task, all forms of hallucination augmentation consistently degraded performance, suggesting that augmentation utility is highly sensitive to the alignment between the perturbation and the task's knowledge requirements.

Our random-permutation semantic control (C5) provides evidence that the observed gains on BBBP are not attributable solely to scientific writing style, as disrupting molecule-description alignment eliminates discriminative improvement while preserving linguistic register.

These findings should be interpreted cautiously given the limited scope of this pilot study: two LLM scales, two datasets, and single-split evaluation. We do not propose hallucination augmentation as a practical technique, nor do we claim to have identified the causal mechanism underlying the observed effects. Rather, this work aims to provide a structured empirical characterization that can guide future investigation into when and why semantic perturbations affect LLM-based molecular reasoning.

\paragraph{Future directions.} Key extensions include: (1) investigation of the distributional compression phenomenon on additional enzymatic datasets, (2) expansion of multi-model validation across diverse LLM architectures and scales, (3) larger-scale evaluation with cross-validation, and (4) comparison with fine-tuned and traditional ML baselines to contextualize the practical significance of the observed effects.
