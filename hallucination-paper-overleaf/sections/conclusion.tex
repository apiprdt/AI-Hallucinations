\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we introduced a controlled semantic perturbation framework to evaluate how the semantic orientation of text influences LLM-based molecular property prediction behavior. Our results reveal a robust task-dependent divergence in semantic sensitivity: enzymatic reasoning (BACE) and solubility tasks (ESOL) are fragile to textual augmentation (including both factual descriptors and semantic fabrication), characterized by a systematic collapse of discriminative capacity and calibration on BACE (which we term ``distributional compression'') and corresponding directional degradation on ESOL. This compression is marked by severe entropy collapse ($H < 0.7$) and associated with high distributional divergence, systematically forcing predictions into a narrow, non-informative confidence state. We note that high $D_{KL}$ alone is not diagnostic of compression: conditions without entropy collapse (e.g., C2b) exhibit comparable $D_{KL}$ values without significant AUC degradation. Conversely, physicochemical prediction (BBBP) exhibits directional sensitivity to structural topic focus for the 8B model, where semantic alignment appears to modulate performance without destroying prediction entropy. On BACE, this alignment effect is superseded by the distributional compression phenomenon for most augmented conditions; notably, however, prompt-constrained structural augmentation (C4a) preserved baseline discriminability and even improved calibration (ECE: 0.069 vs.\ 0.151), indicating that the compression effect is not universal across all forms of semantic perturbation but is specific to conditions that induce entropy collapse. Crucially, we find that the \textit{semantic orientation} induced by a prompt can drive predictive shifts even in the absence of factual taxonomic correctness, suggesting that textual augmentations function as a form of semantic conditioning.

These findings indicate that text-conditioned generation is highly sensitive to the structural claims and topical focus of the prompt, providing a methodological probe for investigating inference behavior. We observe that prompt-conditioned generation did not reliably yield taxonomy-consistent outputs, which underscores the challenge of enforcing specific semantic perturbation categories in generative chemistry systems and suggests that the taxonomy is best utilized as a perturbation design scaffold rather than a validated semantic ontology. This study provides a structured methodological foundation for future work investigating the boundary between linguistic register and semantic utility in text-conditioned molecular prediction.

\subsubsection*{Future directions.} Key extensions include: (1) investigation of the distributional compression phenomenon on additional enzymatic datasets, (2) expansion of multi-model validation across diverse LLM architectures and scales, (3) larger-scale evaluation with cross-validation, and (4) comparison with fine-tuned LLM models and additional traditional ML methods to further contextualize the practical significance of the observed effects.
