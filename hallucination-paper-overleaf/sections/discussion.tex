\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We organize this discussion by explicitly separating empirical observations from interpretive speculation, and by identifying alternative explanations where appropriate.

\subsection{Distributional Bias in Hallucination Generation}
\paragraph{Observation.} Across both datasets, naturally generated hallucinations (C3) are overwhelmingly classified as Contextual Confabulation (CC, $\sim$80\%), with Structural Phantom (SP) accounting for only 1.3--2.4\% of outputs. Despite this, forced SP hallucinations (C4a) exhibited the highest ROC-AUC on BBBP.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} We refer to this distributional asymmetry as the ``Lazy Architect'' pattern: the model preferentially generates generic, plausible scientific narratives rather than structurally specific perturbations. This labeling is purely descriptive and does not imply a causal mechanism within the model architecture.

\paragraph{Alternative explanations.} The low SP rate may reflect prompt design rather than an intrinsic model bias. Different prompt formulations or higher temperatures might elicit more structurally specific outputs. Additionally, our rule-based classifier may undercount SP instances if the generated text uses structural language not captured by our keyword set.

\subsection{Task-Dependent Asymmetry}
\paragraph{Observation.} On BBBP, hallucination augmentation (C3, C4a--c) improved ROC-AUC relative to baseline. On BACE, all hallucination conditions degraded performance, with C3 reducing ROC-AUC from 0.656 to 0.488. The BACE prediction distribution collapsed from a bimodal pattern to a narrow unimodal distribution centered near $p \approx 0.17$.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} We describe the BACE phenomenon as ``prediction collapse''---a systematic compression of predicted probabilities that destroys discriminative signal. One possible explanation is that LLM priors for physicochemical properties (relevant to BBB permeability) are better calibrated than priors for enzymatic inhibition (BACE-1), leading to constructive perturbation in the former case and destructive perturbation in the latter.

\paragraph{Alternative explanations.} The BACE performance degradation may also reflect: (1) dataset-specific class imbalance interacting with the hallucination text, (2) the LLM's pre-training corpus containing more BBB-related than BACE-related molecular knowledge, or (3) differences in molecular complexity between the two datasets affecting how the model processes augmented prompts. We cannot distinguish among these explanations with the current experimental design.

\subsection{Semantic Content vs.\ Stylistic Priming}
\paragraph{Observation.} The circular-shift control (C5) preserved scientific register while disrupting molecule-description semantic alignment. C5 produced no improvement over baseline (ROC-AUC 0.530 vs.\ 0.533 for C0), despite using text that is linguistically indistinguishable from C3 hallucinations.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} This dissociation suggests that the discriminative gains observed under C3 are not attributable solely to scientific writing style or prompt length, but depend on some degree of semantic relationship between the description and the target molecule.

\paragraph{Caveat.} The C5 control uses a simple circular shift ($i \to i+1$), which means adjacent molecules in the dataset receive nearly-adjacent hallucinations. If structurally similar molecules are adjacent (e.g., due to dataset ordering), C5 may retain partial semantic relevance. A fully randomized permutation would provide a stronger control, though we note that our datasets use scaffold-split ordering which reduces this risk.

\subsection{What This Study Does Not Establish}
To prevent overinterpretation, we explicitly note the following non-claims:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We do not establish a \emph{causal mechanism} for why hallucinations affect LLM predictions. Our results are correlational within the evaluated framework.
    \item We do not claim that SP hallucinations are universally superior. This observation is limited to a single physicochemical task (BBBP) with a single model.
    \item We do not propose hallucination augmentation as a practical method for molecular property prediction. The observed effects may be artifacts of the zero-shot evaluation setting.
    \item We do not claim that the taxonomy is exhaustive. The CC category is a residual class, and finer-grained taxonomies may reveal additional structure.
\end{itemize}
