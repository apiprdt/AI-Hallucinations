\section{Discussion}

The results demonstrate that LLM-based molecular property prediction is sensitive to structured semantic perturbations, with the direction and magnitude of the effect being highly dependent on the task domain.

\subsection{Taxonomy Compliance and Prompt Sensitivity}
\subsubsection*{Observation.} Post-hoc verification revealed a significant gap between prompt design intent and the actual taxonomy of generated hallucinations. In particular, the Structural Phantom (SP) operator (C4a) failed to elicit specific structural fabrications in our sample (0\% compliance), despite being associated with the largest directional ROC-AUC shift on BBBP. Conversely, Property Inversion (C4b) and Mechanism Fabrication (C4c) showed higher compliance rates ($\sim$20--25\%).

\subsubsection*{Alternative Explanations.} The low SP rate may reflect prompt design interactions rather than an intrinsic model bias; different prompt formulations or higher temperatures might elicit more structurally specific outputs. Additionally, our rule-based classifier may conservatively undercount SP instances if the generated text employs structural synonyms not captured by our heuristic keyword dictionary.

\subsection{Semantic Orientation vs.\ Factual Accuracy}
\subsubsection*{Observation.} A key finding of this study is the divergence between prompt intent and factuality in the C4a (Structural) condition. Despite achieving the highest directional ROC-AUC on BBBP, post-hoc verification revealed a 0\% Structural Phantom classification rate for C4a. The model generated structurally-focused contextual descriptions rather than specific structural fabrications.

\subsubsection*{Interpretation.} This suggests that the \textit{semantic orientation} of the perturbation---the topical focus induced by the prompt---may be a more potent driver of performance shifts than the factual taxonomic correctness of the generated text. We characterize this phenomenon as a form of \textit{informative hallucination}, where the model's response to structured textual modifications reveals latent chemical associations that are statistically relevant to the prediction task, even when they do not conform to intended factual categories. This indicates that LLMs may benefit from semantic regularization through structured prompts that anchor the model's latent state to task-relevant chemical domains. These effects resemble calibration-sensitive shifts in confidence structure rather than simple ranking perturbations, suggesting that semantic augmentation modulates the model's internal probability density.

\subsection{Task-Dependent Asymmetry}
\subsubsection*{Observation.} On BBBP, hallucination augmentation (C3, C4a--c) was associated with directionally improved ROC-AUC relative to the SMILES-only baseline (C0: 0.533). Notably, the non-chemical gibberish condition (C2b, 0.553) yielded a higher ROC-AUC than purely factual augmentation (C1, 0.541). On the BACE test set ($N=152$), a contrasting and highly robust pattern was observed. The baseline (C0, Seed 42) yielded a ROC-AUC of 0.674 [0.592, 0.759], while the multi-seed mean was slightly lower at 0.644 (Table~\ref{tab:robustness}). Unlike the exploratory trends on BBBP, nearly all hallucination-augmented conditions on BACE produced statistically significant and substantial performance degradation: free hallucination (C3) reduced ROC-AUC to 0.504 [0.420, 0.592] ($p = 0.016$), C4b to 0.542 ($p = 0.011$), and C4c to 0.566 ($p = 0.083$). The notable exception remained C4a (structural augmentation), which largely preserved baseline performance (0.666, $p = 0.808$).

\subsubsection*{Interpretation.} This task-dependent asymmetry suggests that augmentation utility is sensitive to the alignment between perturbation content and task knowledge requirements. The LLM's pre-training corpus likely encodes more robust representations of general physicochemical properties (relevant to BBBP) than specific enzymatic mechanisms (BACE), leading to constructive perturbation in the former and destructive interference in the latter.

\subsubsection*{Alternative Explanations.} We emphasize that these observed shifts on BBBP do not demonstrate that hallucinations enhance genuine molecular reasoning. The improvements may partially reflect latent statistical correlations introduced by textual perturbation that happen to align with task-relevant physicochemical descriptors in the BBBP evaluation set. The C2b result noted above (where gibberish outperforms factual context) further suggests that the LLM may be responding to prompt length, formatting, or linguistic complexity, rather than extracting utility solely from relevant semantic content. This implies that textual context, regardless of factual utility, may serve as a stylistic anchor that influences the model's latent state. While textual perturbations induce directional shifts in raw prediction scores, none of the observed ROC-AUC improvements on the BBBP dataset reached statistical significance ($p > 0.05$). The BACE degradation, by contrast, was statistically significant across most conditions.

\subsection{Semantic Content vs.\ Stylistic Priming}
\subsubsection*{Observation.} The random-permutation control (C5) preserved scientific register while disrupting molecule-description semantic alignment. C5 produced no discriminative improvement over baseline (ROC-AUC 0.481 vs.\ 0.533 for C0 on BBBP), despite utilizing text that is linguistically indistinguishable from C3 hallucinations.

\subsubsection*{Interpretation.} This suggests that the observed performance shifts are not merely artifacts of scientific register or stylistic priming. Rather, the molecule-description alignment---the semantic link between the SMILES input and the textual augmentation---is necessary for the perturbation to influence prediction behavior. This supports our hypothesis that structured perturbations interact with the model's latent representation of the molecule.
