\textbf{Background:} This work addresses these gaps through a controlled perturbation framework to characterize \emph{architecture-dependent sensitivity} to structured molecular fabrications. We make the following scoping explicit: we do not propose a novel architecture for molecular prediction, nor do we claim to mechanistically explain why hallucinations alter LLM reasoning. Instead, we empirically characterize how model capacity and task domain interact with specific types of structured semantic perturbations.

The primary contribution of this work is the characterization of architecture-dependent hallucination sensitivity through a controlled semantic perturbation framework. We define a lightweight categorization scheme—Structural Phantom (SP), Property Inversion (PI), Mechanism Fabrication (MF), and Contextual Confabulation (CC)—utilized as controlled perturbation operators to isolate specific semantic effects across nine conditions and two model scales (Llama-3.1-8B and Llama-3.3-70B).

\textbf{Results:} We identify a task-dependent divergence and evidence of architecture-dependent sensitivity: on BBBP, Contextual Confabulation (CC) is associated with performance shifts for the 8B model, while the 70B model exhibits reversed sensitivity. On BACE, all scales exhibit consistent distributional compression toward chance level. A random-permutation control confirms that these effects are semantically grounded rather than driven by stylistic priming.

\textbf{Conclusions:} These findings suggest that LLM-based molecular reasoning is sensitive to structured semantic perturbations in an architecture-dependent manner, highlighting the importance of scale-aware evaluation when using generative augmentations in scientific pipelines.
