\textbf{Background:} Recent studies suggest that large language model (LLM) hallucinations may influence molecular property prediction, yet the nature of this effect remains poorly characterized across different hallucination types and model scales.

\textbf{Methods:} We introduce a controlled semantic perturbation framework with three prompt-constrained perturbation operators---Structural Phantom (SP), Property Inversion (PI), Mechanism Fabrication (MF)---alongside a residual Contextual Confabulation (CC) category. We evaluate nine conditions on two MoleculeNet benchmarks (BBBP, $N=204$; BACE, $N=152$) using multi-seed evaluation (Llama-3.1-8B, $n=3$ seeds) and cross-model validation (Llama-3.3-70B). A random-permutation control isolates semantic content from stylistic priming.

\textbf{Results:} On BBBP, prompt-constrained structural augmentation (C4a) yields the largest observed ROC-AUC increase (0.626 vs.\ 0.533 baseline), consistent across 97.6\% of bootstrap resamples, though none of the BBBP shifts reached statistical significance after correction ($p > 0.05$). Post-hoc verification reveals that C4a outputs are predominantly classified as Contextual Confabulation (0\% SP), indicating the effect reflects structural topic focus rather than hallucination type. The 70B model exhibits reversed sensitivity (C3: 0.549 vs.\ 0.644 baseline). On BACE, most augmentation conditions produce significant performance degradation with distributional compression toward chance level, though C4a (structural) largely preserves baseline performance. The random-permutation control indicates that the observed effects depend on semantic content rather than scientific register alone.

\textbf{Conclusions:} LLM-based molecular predictions exhibit differential sensitivity to structured semantic perturbations that varies by task domain and model capacity. These findings underscore the need for controlled evaluation when deploying generative augmentations in scientific reasoning pipelines.
