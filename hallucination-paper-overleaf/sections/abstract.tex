Synthetic semantic perturbations generated by large language models (LLMs) have been observed to influence molecular property prediction, yet the underlying nature of this effect remains poorly characterized across different task regimes. We introduce a controlled semantic perturbation framework to evaluate how structured modifications --- which we characterize as synthetic semantic priors --- influence zero-shot prediction. We evaluate sembilan conditions across empat MoleculeNet benchmarks covering both classification (BBBP, BACE, Tox21) and regression (ESOL) tasks, using multi-seed evaluation (Llama-3.1-8B) and cross-model validation (Llama-3.3-70B). Our results reveal a consistent task-dependent sensitivity: while structural topic focus (C4a) yields directional ROC-AUC improvements in physicochemical (BBBP) and toxicity (Tox21) domains, semantic fabrication robustly degrades performance and probability calibration on enzymatic (BACE) and solubility (ESOL) tasks. We demonstrate that the \textit{semantic orientation} of a perturbation, rather than its factual accuracy alone, is a primary driver of ini shifts, inducing calibration-sensitive behavior resembling the activation of latent semantic priors. Ini findings indicate that LLM-based molecular predictions exhibit systematic sensitivity to structured perturbations, highlighting the role of controlled evaluation in understanding the reliability of text-conditioned scientific reasoning pipelines.
