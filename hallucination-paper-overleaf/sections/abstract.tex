Recent studies suggest that large language model (LLM) hallucinations can occasionally improve downstream molecular property prediction. However, the specific factors enabling this effect remain uncharacterized. In this exploratory study, we propose a heuristic, operational taxonomy of hallucination types---Structural Phantom (SP), Property Inversion (PI), Mechanism Fabrication (MF), and Contextual Confabulation (CC)---and evaluate their impact through a nine-condition perturbation analysis on two classification benchmarks (BBBP, BACE) using Llama-3.1-8B. On the BBBP task ($N=204$), SP hallucinations yielded the highest observed ROC-AUC (0.626 vs.\ 0.533 SMILES-only baseline). Conversely, on the BACE task ($N=152$), hallucination conditions degraded performance and exhibited severe distributional compression toward negative predictions. A circular-shift control condition preserved scientific tone while breaking semantic alignment, yielding baseline-level performance (0.530) and indicating that semantic content, rather than stylistic priming, influences prediction outcomes within this evaluated setting. Analysis of unprompted hallucinations revealed a distributional bias toward CC ($\sim$80\%), despite the rare SP category exhibiting the strongest predictive association on BBBP. These findings suggest that hallucination utility is highly sensitive to both task domain and specific hallucination types. We explicitly bound these observations to the evaluated single-model, limited-sample setting, emphasizing that LLM hallucinations should not be treated as a monolithic phenomenon.
