Prior work indicates that LLM-generated hallucinations can influence molecular property prediction, yet the nature of this interaction remains insufficiently characterized. In this exploratory study, we introduce a controlled semantic perturbation framework to systematically evaluate how structured hallucination types influence predictive outcomes under bounded experimental conditions. We utilize an operational categorization scheme—comprising Structural Phantom (SP), Property Inversion (PI), Mechanism Fabrication (MF), and Contextual Confabulation (CC) types—and conduct a nine-condition ablation study on the BBBP and BACE benchmarks using Llama-3.1-8B. Our results reveal a pronounced task-dependent sensitivity: while structured perturbations are associated with directional improvement on the BBBP physicochemical task, similar augmentations lead to systematic performance degradation and prediction distribution collapse on the BACE enzymatic task. A circular-shift control preserved scientific register while disrupting semantic alignment, yielding near-baseline performance and suggesting that semantic content, rather than stylistic priming, is the primary driver of these effects. Analysis of unprompted hallucinations reveals a generative bias toward generic confabulations (~80\%), despite the rarer SP category exhibiting the strongest predictive association on BBBP. We explicitly bound these observations to the evaluated single-model, limited-sample setting, emphasizing that LLM-augmented prediction sensitivity depends on the alignment between perturbation type and task domain.
